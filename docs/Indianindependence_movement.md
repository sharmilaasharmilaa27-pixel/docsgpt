# Indian Independence Movement

## Introduction

The Indian Independence Movement was a long political and social struggle against British colonial rule. The movement involved leaders, political organizations, students, workers, farmers, writers, reformers, and ordinary citizens across different regions of India.

The struggle developed over many decades and included constitutional methods, political campaigns, public protests, revolutionary activities, economic boycotts, and mass movements.

India became independent on 15 August 1947.

## Establishment of British Rule

British political influence in India expanded significantly after the Battle of Plassey in 1757 and the Battle of Buxar in 1764. The British East India Company gradually gained control over large areas of the subcontinent.

After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the British government transferred administration from the East India Company to the British Crown.

Colonial policies affected Indian political institutions, agriculture, trade, industries, and social life.

## Indian Rebellion of 1857

The Indian Rebellion of 1857 was a major uprising against the East India Company's rule.

The rebellion involved soldiers as well as sections of the civilian population. Important figures associated with the uprising included Rani Lakshmibai, Bahadur Shah II, Nana Sahib, Tantia Tope, and others.

Although the rebellion was suppressed, it became an important historical event in the development of Indian resistance to colonial rule.

## Formation of the Indian National Congress

The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885.

During its early period, many Congress leaders used constitutional methods to demand political representation and administrative reforms.

Leaders associated with the early nationalist movement included Dadabhai Naoroji, Surendranath Banerjee, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, and others.

Dadabhai Naoroji developed the Drain of Wealth theory, arguing that economic resources were being transferred from India to Britain under colonial rule.

## Partition of Bengal

In 1905, the British government announced the partition of Bengal.

The decision generated strong opposition and contributed to the Swadeshi Movement. Indians promoted locally produced goods and boycotted some British goods.

The Swadeshi movement became an important example of using economic action as a form of political resistance.

## Rise of Mass Nationalism

The early twentieth century saw the growth of more assertive nationalist politics.

Leaders such as Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, and Lala Lajpat Rai became associated with a more forceful approach to political mobilization.

At the same time, revolutionary organizations developed in several parts of India.

## Mahatma Gandhi

Mahatma Gandhi became one of the most influential leaders of the Indian independence movement.

He emphasized nonviolent resistance, civil disobedience, mass participation, and political action based on moral principles.

Gandhi's approach helped bring large sections of the population into the national movement.

## Non-Cooperation Movement

The Non-Cooperation Movement began in 1920.

The movement encouraged Indians to withdraw cooperation from colonial institutions and boycott certain British goods and institutions.

The movement became a major nationwide campaign but was suspended by Gandhi after the Chauri Chaura incident in 1922, in which a violent clash resulted in the deaths of police personnel.

## Civil Disobedience Movement

The Civil Disobedience Movement became a major phase of the independence struggle.

One of its most famous events was the Salt March of 1930.

Gandhi and his followers marched to the coastal village of Dandi and challenged the colonial salt laws.

The Salt March attracted international attention and demonstrated the potential of peaceful mass resistance.

## Quit India Movement

The Quit India Movement was launched in August 1942.

The movement demanded an end to British rule in India.

The slogan "Do or Die" became strongly associated with the movement. Many leaders were arrested, but protests and underground activities continued in different parts of the country.

## Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army

Subhas Chandra Bose was an important nationalist leader who believed that military action could play a role in achieving independence.

He became associated with the Indian National Army, which sought to fight British rule with Japanese support during the Second World War.

The INA trials after the war generated significant public interest and nationalist sentiment in India.

## Role of Women

Women played important roles in India's independence movement.

Figures such as Sarojini Naidu, Aruna Asaf Ali, Kasturba Gandhi, Sucheta Kriplani, and Rani Gaidinliu participated in different forms of political and social activity.

Women participated in protests, political organizations, underground activities, campaigns, and public mobilization.

## Role of Students and Workers

Students and workers participated actively in nationalist campaigns.

Students organized demonstrations and political activities, while workers participated in strikes and labor movements.

These groups helped transform nationalism from a primarily elite political activity into a broader social movement.

## Road to Independence

The Second World War significantly changed the political situation.

Britain faced economic and military pressures, while demands for Indian independence became increasingly difficult to ignore.

Negotiations between British authorities and Indian political leaders continued through the 1940s.

The Indian Independence Act 1947 provided the legal framework for the creation of two independent dominions, India and Pakistan.

## Independence and Partition

India became independent on 15 August 1947.

The process of independence was accompanied by the Partition of British India and the creation of Pakistan.

Partition resulted in large-scale population movements and severe communal violence.

The independence of India therefore represents both the achievement of freedom from colonial rule and a period of immense human suffering.

## Legacy

The independence movement shaped India's democratic institutions, political culture, constitutional development, and national identity.

The Constitution of India came into effect on 26 January 1950.

The movement remains an important part of Indian history because it demonstrates the impact of political organization, mass participation, social reform, and resistance to colonial rule.

## Key Takeaways

- The independence movement developed over several decades.
- The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885.
- The Swadeshi Movement followed the 1905 partition of Bengal.
- Gandhi promoted nonviolent mass movements.
- The Non-Cooperation Movement began in 1920.
- The Salt March was an important event of the Civil Disobedience Movement.
- The Quit India Movement began in 1942.
- India became independent on 15 August 1947.
- Independence was accompanied by the Partition of British India.
